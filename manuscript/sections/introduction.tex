\section{Introduction}
Periodic sensing and control traffic poses an energy--reliability tradeoff in
shared uplinks. A device can lower its power and accumulate service across
several slots before a deadline, as motivated by classical energy-efficient
packet scheduling on single and multiple-access links~\cite{lazy2002,uysal2004}. However, extending transmissions increases
the opportunities for interference. With heterogeneous periods and unaligned
arrival phases, distributed devices must decide which subchannel and power
to use, and when to remain silent despite a pending packet.
The resulting energy--reliability tradeoff calls for per-device delivery
constraints and energy references that account for interference.

Deadline-constrained scheduling characterizes per-client delivery
requirements~\cite{hou2010} and periodic traffic with general offsets~\cite{deng2017},
but relies on a central scheduler; delay-constrained grant-free access has been
treated with distributed pilot and power selection~\cite{bai2025}.
Recurrent policies with local acknowledgments have been used for distributed
spectrum access~\cite{naparstek2019}, and vehicular MARL jointly selects
subbands and powers under payload deadlines~\cite{liang2019}.
Reliability-constrained power control for periodic mission-critical
transmissions~\cite{guo2023} and recurrent spectrum/power allocation in
multicell networks~\cite{zhang2025} further motivate this setting.
The close XR-SBPC study~\cite{bagherinejad2025} combines periodic traffic
with offsets, accumulated Shannon service, and subband--power--silence actions.
It optimizes reliability in downlink subnetworks, using neighboring actions
and current link gains. We study transmit-energy minimization with per-device
delivery requirements in a common-receiver uplink, using only local packet
history and delayed AP feedback.

We develop a distributed controller with a shared, subchannel-equivariant
recurrent actor trained by multi-agent proximal policy optimization
(MAPPO)~\cite{yu2022}. A fixed violation penalty
is compared with local dual adaptation inspired by state-augmented
constrained reinforcement learning~\cite{calvo2024}. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A causal distributed access formulation for asynchronous periodic
packets, with accumulated service and joint channel, power, and silence decisions.
\item Centralized zero-loss energy references for the same discrete-power
model: an exact solution when $N\le K$, and lower and feasible upper
references when devices outnumber subchannels.
\item Held-out evaluation against calibrated local rules and an XR-SBPC-style
SAC adaptation. Multiple seeds, paired intervals, and a fresh confirmation
set quantify reward effects within SAC and the energy advantage of the
MAPPO scheme. Transmission statistics reveal lower power and reduced
harmful overlap in the learned access patterns.
\end{enumerate}
