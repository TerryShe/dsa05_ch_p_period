\section{Experimental Evaluation}\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Scenario and evaluation protocol}
Table~\ref{tab:parameters} gives the scenario and training parameters.
Positions are uniform by area in a disk, with gains
$g_i=10^{-4}\max(d_i/1\,\mathrm{m},1)^{-4}$, and remain fixed within
an episode. Periods and packet sizes give equal offered load per device.
Traffic-class counts for $N=4,6,8,12$ are $(2,1,1)$, $(2,2,2)$,
$(3,3,2)$, and $(4,4,4)$, respectively.

For each $N$, we train separate policies with seeds 101, 202, and 303,
drawing each episode from one million training instances. Development,
training, validation, test, and confirmation pools are disjoint.
The study assesses generalization to new topologies and phases at a trained
$N$. The MAPPO entropy coefficient decreases from 0.01 to zero over ten
million slots; minibatches contain four environment trajectories.
The penalty and dual step size were selected in a two-million-slot study
at $N=8$ and transferred to the long runs.

Validation uses 64 instances, one stochastic execution each, every million
training slots. An episode is feasible when all devices satisfy
$V_i\le\lfloor0.05M_i\rfloor$, where $M_i$ and $V_i$ count evaluated
packets and violations. Let $F$ be the fraction of feasible episodes.
Among checkpoints with validation $F\ge0.95$, selection minimizes mean
$E/U_0$; otherwise it maximizes $F$, breaking ties by energy.
MAPPO-D jointly selects its initial multiplier. Both selected and final
checkpoints are evaluated, with selected checkpoints providing the main results.

Testing uses 200 unseen instances per $N$, two stochastic executions each,
and all three training seeds. Private device random streams use common seed
assignments across methods. Evaluated packets have deadlines in $(200,2000]$;
$E$ includes their full lifetime transmit energy, including failed packets,
matching the centralized packet set. Mean power instead counts transmissions
in slots $[200,2000)$. A method meets the feasibility criterion when the
point estimate of aggregate $F$ is at least 0.95. We compare energy among
methods meeting this criterion and calculate percentage savings from their
mean normalized energies. We use 10\,000 two-level bootstrap resamples of
training seeds and instances, averaging repetitions within an instance. Method differences
resample instances jointly and training seeds independently. All reported
intervals are 95\% percentile intervals.

\subsection{Local rules and held-out performance}
\emph{Random-max} sends on a random subchannel at maximum power.
\emph{Sticky-max} retains its subchannel when previous service meets the
current pace $s_i/D_i$, otherwise reselecting randomly, at maximum power.
\emph{Lazy-pacing} uses its latest link estimate to choose the lowest power
meeting that pace and changes subchannel when necessary.
The strongest feasible rule, \emph{paced-sticky}, remembers its gain across
packets and retains a subchannel within 3 dB of the minimum interference.
Otherwise it switches with probability 0.5 to one within 3 dB of that minimum.
It selects the lowest power predicted to serve $(1+m)s_i/D_i$ bits,
probing at maximum power until its gain is known. Validation calibration over
$m\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8,16,32\}$ selects $0,0.25,0.5,8$ for the
four loads. All rules obey the same execution information boundary.

Maximum-power rules are reliable but expensive; lazy-pacing fails the
feasibility criterion at every load.

\subsection{Comparison with an XR-SBPC-style learner}\label{sec:sac}
We adapt the discrete soft actor--critic (SAC) scheme of
XR-SBPC~\cite{bagherinejad2025} to the same environment and actor features.
Shared feedforward actor and twin local Q networks use widths
400/300/200/100, LeakyReLU in the first three layers and tanh in the last.
The learning rate is $10^{-4}$, batch size 512, replay capacity 10\,000,
target update rate 0.005, and target entropy 0.05, with initial temperature 1.
Proportional prioritized replay uses exponent 0.6 and importance exponent
annealed from 0.4 to 1. One update follows each step of 16 environments,
starting when the buffer is full. We evaluate discounts of 0.5 and 0.99.

Three rewards distinguish the objective from the training scheme.
\emph{SAC-V1} gives a device $+50$ on completion and $-100$ on expiry.
Otherwise, while a packet is pending, it receives $5b_i/(s_i/D_i)$ if
$b_i\ge s_i/D_i$, and $-10$ if below that pace. Idle reward is zero.
\emph{SAC-V1s} retains only completion and expiry rewards.
\emph{SAC-V2} uses exactly MAPPO-F's team reward~\eqref{eq:reward},
including on idle transitions. All variants train Q values on idle
transitions, but exclude them from actor and temperature losses, avoiding
bootstrap from untrained idle states. Neighbor actions and instantaneous
channel gains available in the original study are omitted to match our
execution information.

All $3$ rewards $\times$ $2$ discounts $\times$ $3$ seeds receive the same
20-million-slot budget as MAPPO. Following the cross-load tests, we compare
the frozen policies on an independent confirmation set, C1: 200 new
instances, two executions each, with separately computed references.
Checkpoint selection and analysis rules were fixed before C1 evaluation;
the existing MAPPO policies were frozen. Reward comparisons fix
$\gamma=0.99$ in advance. For the comparison of complete schemes, validation
on seed 101 selects one discount per reward, applied to all three seeds:
0.5 for V1/V1s and 0.99 for V2.

\subsection{Access behavior and reliability}
Selected MAPPO-F and MAPPO-D satisfy the feasibility criterion at every $N$,
but their paired energy intervals all contain zero. At $N=8$, means are
1.213 and 1.463, with difference interval $[-0.737,0.005]$; C1 gives
$[-0.734,0.003]$.
Final MAPPO-F checkpoints remain feasible at all loads ($F=0.987$--0.992),
whereas final MAPPO-D drops to 0.915 and 0.938 at $N=8,12$.
The fixed penalty is therefore our simpler primary configuration.

Co-channel overlap is the fraction of transmissions sharing a subchannel
with another active device. Interference loss is one minus total
actual mutual information divided by the interference-free service of the
same transmissions. For MAPPO-F, short (2M) and long (20M) configurations
reduce overlap from 0.712 to 0.211 and loss from 0.418 to 0.064
(Fig.~\ref{fig:coordination}). Their test energies are 2.321 and 1.213.
The long configuration extends both training and entropy annealing.
Explicit interference histories at lags 10/20/40 did not reach a predeclared
5\% improvement threshold in a separate short-budget validation experiment.

At $\gamma=0.99$, SAC-V2 sends in nearly every slot with a pending packet,
with overlap 0.58--0.68 and interference loss 0.38--0.40 across seeds.
MAPPO-F's corresponding conditional transmission fraction is 0.61--0.76;
82--85\% of its transmissions use 5 or 10 dBm. V1/V1s instead have mean
completion times of 0.16--0.27 of a period, often transmitting at high power.
MAPPO-F combines low-power service with selective silence.
No SAC run beats paced-sticky's validation energy while meeting $F\ge0.95$;
MAPPO-F first does so after 1--4 million slots.

Pooling selected MAPPO-F/D over four loads gives 128 infeasible executions
out of 9600. The worst device is 75--100 m from the AP in 108 of these,
and has period 40 in 83. Such devices have only 45 evaluated packets and
can miss at most two. A MAPPO-F execution at $N=4$ reaches device violation
rate 0.733.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
Experiments use static, frequency-flat channels, exact feedback, and ideal
accumulated service, and count radiated transmit energy. Fading,
finite-blocklength coding, coding gaps, and feedback, circuit, and processing
costs remain extensions. The finite-episode feasibility criterion provides
empirical reliability evidence, without a per-instance constraint guarantee.
Centralized energy references impose zero loss; their ratios do not bound
optimality under the 5\% violation allowance. Results use three training
seeds, and the XR-SBPC-style adaptation covers $N=8$ and two discounts with
transferred hyperparameters. The learning comparison assesses complete
training schemes. Controlled ablations are needed to distinguish architectural
and training contributions, the roles of power adaptation and silence,
and the joint effects of training budget and entropy scheduling.
