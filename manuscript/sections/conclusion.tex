\section{Conclusion}
For the studied periodic uplink, a shared recurrent MAPPO policy learned
useful distributed access behavior from delayed AP feedback and private
packet state. At loads exceeding the number of subchannels, the fixed-penalty
variant substantially reduced transmit energy relative to calibrated local
rules while meeting the empirical per-device feasibility criterion.
Centralized zero-loss references expose both recovery of the low-load
reference and a growing energy gap at higher load. Local dual adaptation
did not yield a statistically resolved energy advantage, and observed
coordination required long training. Literature-based learning comparisons,
controlled mechanism ablations, and robustness to imperfect feedback and
time-varying channels are the next steps needed to extend these conclusions.
