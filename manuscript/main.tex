\begin{abstract}
Periodic uplink devices can save transmit energy by using lower power over
several slots, but overlapping transmissions can erase this benefit when
devices independently select their resources. We study distributed subchannel,
power, and silence decisions for heterogeneous periodic packets with asynchronous
arrival phases and per-device deadline-violation requirements. Each device uses
its own packet state, delayed public received-power measurements, and private
mutual-information feedback; it observes neither other devices' actions nor
their queues. A shared recurrent policy is trained with multi-agent proximal
policy optimization and evaluated against calibrated local rules and centralized
zero-loss energy references. On 200 held-out topology--phase instances per network
size, with three training seeds and two executions per instance, the fixed-penalty
policy reduces mean normalized transmit energy by \SavingSix\%, \SavingEight\%,
and \SavingTwelve\% relative to the strongest evaluated feasible local rule for
6, 8, and 12 devices sharing four subchannels. Its feasible-episode fraction is
at least 0.984 at these loads. With four devices, its energy is within 0.7\%
of the exact zero-loss centralized reference. Validation diagnostics associate
the savings with reduced co-channel overlap and interference loss. Local dual
adaptation gives no statistically resolved energy advantage over the fixed
penalty, while substantial training remains necessary. These results quantify
the benefits and remaining limits of learned access under the specified local
information model.
\end{abstract}
